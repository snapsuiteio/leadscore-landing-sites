Get a finished job ready to invoice

For billing and operations: collect everything the office needs before an invoice is prepared.

Read the steps
  1. Identify the job, the client and the person responsible.
  2. Record what was requested, what was completed and what remains.
  3. Collect labour, materials, agreed rates and any authorized extra charges.
  4. Check who approved any extra work, and whether the quote matches the work.
  5. Find the completion note, photos or service report.
  6. Hand off to one invoice preparer with a review date and a clear status.

The full checklist includes a worked example, a prompt you can copy, and a seven-day pilot.

Follow up with one lead, properly

For sales: one owner, one next step and a date, with a person checking every message before it goes out.

Read the steps
  1. Identify the lead and where they came from.
  2. Check you have permission to contact them, and how. If not, stop.
  3. Record the last conversation and anything you promised.
  4. Name one owner for the next step.
  5. Set the next step and its date.
  6. If there is a proposal, check the scope, prices, dates and terms first.
  7. Draft a short follow-up that asks one clear question.
  8. The owner reviews and sends it personally. Nothing is sent automatically.

Follow-up doesn’t guarantee a sale. The full checklist includes a worked example and a prompt you can copy.

Speak it, then organize it

A practical AI workflow: turn voice notes into email drafts and task lists, and organize receipts and invoices for review.

Read the steps
  1. Choose one result: an email draft, a task list or a filing plan.
  2. Use fictional or redacted information while testing.
  3. Use the prompts in the guide. Each one tells the AI not to invent facts or send anything.
  4. Check names, dates, amounts and any implied promise before you use the result.
  5. Let your accountant review accounting and tax decisions.
