Get one completed job ready for billing
A free checklist from Dwain Browne. No sign-up needed.

Choose one job that is finished or nearly finished. Use made-up details if you are testing this with an AI tool. You do not need to upload client records.

1. Identify the job.
   Job reference: ______  Client reference: ______
   Site or equipment reference: ______  Person responsible: ______

2. Record the work.
   What was requested? ______
   What was completed? ______
   What remains? ______
   Completion date: ______

3. Collect the billable details.
   Labour and units: ______
   Materials or parts: ______
   Agreed rates or pricing reference: ______
   Travel or other authorized charges: ______

4. Check changes and approval.
   Was extra work requested? ______
   Who approved it and where is that recorded? ______
   Does the original quote match the work completed? ______

5. Check the supporting record.
   Completion note: ______
   Photos or service report reference: ______
   Client acknowledgement if needed: ______

6. Give the office one clear handoff.
   Invoice preparer: ______
   Missing information owner: ______
   Target review date: ______
   Status: ready for review / missing details / awaiting approval

Worked example using fictional details
Job M-104: routine pump service at Sample Property. The technician reports "serviced pump, replaced seal." Missing: labour time, the seal part reference and approval for the replacement. Next step: the supervisor confirms the missing details before billing review. The worksheet does not infer hours, prices or authorization.

Prompt you can copy
"Review this fictional job note for billing readiness. Create four sections: work completed, labour and materials stated, approvals stated, and missing information. Use only the information provided. Do not invent hours, prices, tax, approvals or invoice totals. Keep unknowns labelled Unknown. Draft a short handoff for the office. Do not create or send an invoice. Job note: [paste sample note]."

Your seven-day pilot
Use this checklist on the next five suitable jobs. Record the time spent collecting missing details and the number of handoffs returned for clarification. Compare with a small baseline from similar jobs. These are your observations, not promised savings.

Before changing software, ask: is the problem missing information, unclear responsibility, an approval delay, or a tool gap? Fix the simplest cause first.
