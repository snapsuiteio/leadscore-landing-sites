Speak it then organize it

Choose one result
Start with an email draft, a task list or a filing plan. Use fictional or redacted information while testing. Do not share passwords, bank details, private employee records or confidential client information with an unapproved tool.
Voice note example
“Sample customer asked when the maintenance visit can happen. We still need to check availability. Draft a short reply saying I am checking and will confirm. Ask which of Tuesday or Wednesday works better. Do not promise either date.”
Prompt: “Turn my spoken notes into a short business email draft. Preserve my meaning. Do not invent dates, prices, commitments or facts. List anything I need to confirm separately. Do not send the message. Notes: [dictated note].”
Example result: “Hi, I’m checking availability for the maintenance visit. Would Tuesday or Wednesday work better for you? I’ll confirm the date once I’ve checked.” Review the wording and the implied commitment before use.
Turn a recap into tasks
Prompt: “Extract the actions from this fictional recap. For each action, list the task, owner and due date only when stated. Otherwise write Confirm. Separate decisions from open questions. Do not assign work or create calendar events. Recap: [dictated recap].”
Organize financial paperwork for review
Prompt: “Using this sample list of receipts and invoices, suggest file names in YYYY-MM-DD_supplier_document-type_reference format. Do not change files. Flag missing dates, duplicate-looking references and unreadable details. Keep the original reference beside each proposal. Do not decide tax treatment, claim deductibility or alter amounts. Return a review list.”
Example: “Receipt_A.pdf, Sample Supplies, 2026-10-02, reference R-105” becomes the proposed name “2026-10-02_Sample-Supplies_receipt_R-105.pdf”. If the date is missing, flag it; do not guess. Confirm any duplicate before deleting anything.
Review before using the result
Check names, dates, amounts, meaning and any implied promise against the source.
Remove confidential details that are not needed. Check tool access and data handling before using real records.
Approve the final email yourself. Preview file changes; keep originals and a reversible record.
Let the accountant review accounting and tax decisions. AI can help prepare and organize information.
Seven-day pilot: repeat one low-risk task five times. Record review time, corrections and whether the output was useful. Stop if the review burden exceeds the benefit or the task requires access you have not approved.
